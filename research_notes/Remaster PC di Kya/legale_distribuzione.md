# Legal/IP Situation and Distribution Options for a PC Port/Remaster of Kya: Dark Lineage

> Research notes, current as of October 2026. **Not legal advice.** Laws and enforcement practice differ by country. Before any public release or monetization, talk to an IP lawyer (ideally in Italy/EU and the US).
> Method note: EUR-Lex returned an empty page to both the fetch tool and curl. The EU Software Directive wording below is therefore paraphrased from memory of the official text and marked as such. Check it against the EUR-Lex link before relying on it.

## Q1. Who owns the Kya: Dark Lineage IP today, and has Atari done anything with it recently?

### Takeaway
All available evidence points to Atari SA (formerly Infogrames) as the most likely owner. The game was developed by Eden Studios, which was a wholly owned Infogrames/Atari subsidiary when Kya shipped in 2003, and Atari published it. The original Eden Games went into judicial liquidation in 2013. The Eden Games that exists now (bought by Animoca Brands in 2022) is a separate company created through a management buyout, and nothing indicates it holds the Kya IP. No public record shows any Kya re-release, remaster or rights deal through October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Kya: Dark Lineage was developed by Eden Games and published by Atari for PS2 only. It released in North America on 18 Nov 2003, in France on 21 Nov 2003 and in PAL territories on 2 Apr 2004. Wikipedia lists no re-release and no sequel — [Wikipedia: Kya: Dark Lineage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kya:_Dark_Lineage)
- Trivia relevant to trademark: Nickelodeon's legal department reportedly vetoed the name "Kya" for the Avatar: The Last Airbender lead (later named Katara) because of this game. This suggests the "Kya" name had some trademark weight in the 2000s — [Wikipedia: Kya: Dark Lineage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kya:_Dark_Lineage)
- Infogrames took full ownership of Eden Studios in April 2002. Wikipedia's Eden article says April and a search snippet said May. In either case this was before Kya shipped, so the game was made inside the Infogrames/Atari group — [Wikipedia: Eden Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Games)
- Atari laid off more than 51 of Eden's 80 employees in 2011. The studio filed for judicial liquidation on 29 Jan 2013 — [Wikipedia: Eden Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Games)
- On 31 Oct 2013, former employees restarted Eden Games as an independent studio, backed by ID Invest and Monster Capital (GT Spirit, Gear.Club). Engine Media was the parent company from 2017 to 2021. Animoca Brands (Hong Kong, blockchain gaming) bought it in April 2022. The Wikipedia article says nothing about who owns the Kya IP — [Wikipedia: Eden Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Games)
- Searches for a Kya re-release or remaster in 2025 or 2026 returned nothing. The game is still PS2-exclusive — [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/kya-dark-lineage/), [Wikipedia: Atari Recharged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Recharged)
- Atari's retro strategy:
  - Atari agreed to buy 100% of Nightdive Studios on 22 Mar 2023 and closed on 15 May 2023. The price was $10M up front, half cash and half shares, plus an earn-out of up to $10M. The deal included Nightdive's owned IP library and its KEX engine. CEO Wade Rosen already owned about 13% of Nightdive — [Atari newsroom](https://atari.com/blogs/newsroom/atari-acquires-nightdive-studios); [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2023/03/atari-to-acquire-nightdive-studios); [AMF filing](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/MKW/2023/05/FCMKW119938_20230515.pdf)
  - Atari bought Digital Eclipse in late 2023 for about $20M as part of its "retro-focused growth strategy." Digital Eclipse says it remains free to work with other publishers — [Time Extension](https://timeextension.com/news/2023/10/atari-is-buying-digital-eclipse); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2023/10/atari-ramps-up-retro-ambitions-with-digital-eclipse-acquisition)
  - Atari also bought the AtariAge archive and the rights to hundreds of 1980s and 1990s games — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/consoles-pc/atari-looking-to-acquire-digital-eclipse-following-nightdive-studios-purchase)
  - In 2025 Atari bought 82% of Thunderful Group for about €4.5M (SteamWorld and more than 20 other IPs) and relaunched the Infogrames label as a publisher — [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2025/07/atari-to-acquire-thunderful-group); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/07/atari-set-to-acquire-82-of-thunderful-group-ab-as-thunderful-announce-theyre-restructuring/); [The Game Business](https://www.thegamebusiness.com/p/can-atari-make-its-retro-games-strategy)

### Inferences
- Kya was made by a wholly owned subsidiary and published by the parent. Under standard work-for-hire and intra-group practice of that era, the copyright in code and assets and the "Kya" trademarks probably sit with Atari SA, or with an Atari entity that inherited Infogrames' assets. This is probable, not confirmed.
- One complication: in 2003 "Atari" was the Infogrames group, which included Atari Inc. (US) and Atari Europe. Later restructurings (Atari Inc.'s 2013 Chapter 11, asset sales) could have moved some titles. An INPI/EUIPO/USPTO trademark search for "KYA" and a direct question to Atari SA are the only ways to confirm.
- Atari is now the most retro-oriented major rights holder in the industry, and it owns two in-house remaster studios (Nightdive, Digital Eclipse) plus a revived Infogrames label. That makes a licensed Kya remaster structurally plausible. It also means Atari would most likely give the work to its own studios instead of licensing it out.
- Licensed music and voice talent contracts from 2003 may not cover new distribution. This is a common obstacle in remasters and would need a rights audit.

### Gaps
- No primary source (trademark register, Atari annual report, catalog list) explicitly naming the current Kya copyright/trademark holder was found. Next steps: search EUIPO eSearch, USPTO TSDR and INPI for "KYA" marks, and review Atari's catalog disclosures in its Universal Registration Document.
- Atari's public stance on fan decompilations of its back catalog was not found. There is also no known Atari takedown against PS2-era fan projects.
- There is no information on whether Kya's music or voice licenses are perpetual.

## Q2. Is decompilation and clean-room reimplementation legal in the EU, Italy and the US?

### Takeaway
In both the EU and the US, decompiling for **interoperability** or **understanding** is a recognized, limited exception. A complete, matching-source decompilation of a game that is then redistributed is a different act. It is copying the program's expression, and none of these exceptions clearly protects it. EU Article 6 explicitly excludes using decompiled information to build a program "substantially similar in its expression."

Projects lower their risk by shipping no original code binaries or assets (the user supplies their own disc or ISO), by avoiding leaked source, and by not charging money. Even so, the decompiled source itself is arguably a derivative of the original code. Risk is reduced, not removed.

### Cited Findings
- **EU – Directive 2009/24/EC (Software Directive)** — [EUR-Lex 32009L0024](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32009L0024). Paraphrased from memory because the fetch failed; please verify.
  - Art. 5(1): the lawful acquirer may reproduce and translate the program when necessary to use it as intended, including to correct errors.
  - Art. 5(3): the lawful user may observe, study and test how the program works in order to find its underlying ideas and principles, while loading or running it.
  - Art. 6(1): reproducing and translating code (decompiling) needs no permission when it is indispensable to get the information needed for interoperability of an *independently created* program. Three conditions apply: it is done by a licensee or lawful user; the information was not already readily available; and it is limited to the parts needed for interoperability.
  - Art. 6(2): the information may not (a) be used for other goals, (b) be given to others unless needed for interoperability, or (c) be used to develop, produce or market a program substantially similar in its expression, or for any other infringing act.
  - Art. 8: contract terms that conflict with Art. 6 or Art. 5(2)-(3) are null and void.
- **CJEU, Top System v Belgian State (C-13/20, 6 Oct 2021).** A lawful acquirer may decompile all or part of a program when needed to correct errors affecting its operation, under Art. 5(1). The Court rejected the argument that Art. 6 is the *only* route to lawful decompilation. Contracts can still regulate this — [SCL](https://www.scl.org/12394-court-of-justice-rules-that-software-buyers-can-decompile-programs-to-correct-errors/); [IPPT](https://www.ippt.eu/items/ippt20211006-cjeu-top-system); [De Bandt](https://www.debandt.eu/en/node/510)
- **Italy.** The directive is implemented in Law 633/1941 (Legge sul diritto d'autore): Art. 64-ter covers the user's rights, including observing and studying the program, and Art. 64-quater covers decompilation for interoperability, with the same "substantially similar" limit. *This comes from background knowledge and was not fetched in this session.* Verify at [Normattiva L. 633/1941](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1941-04-22;633).
- **US – DMCA 17 USC §1201(f).** A person who lawfully obtained a program may circumvent a technological measure "for the sole purpose of identifying and analyzing those elements of the program that are necessary to achieve interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs." Tools and information may be shared only for that purpose — [Cornell LII 17 USC 1201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201)
- **US – Sega v. Accolade (9th Cir. 1992).** Disassembling Sega's object code, including wholesale intermediate copying, was fair use where it was the only way to access unprotected functional ideas (the lockout/compatibility requirements) and the final product was Accolade's own independent games — [Lexplug case brief](https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/sega_enters_ltd_v_accolade_inc__6599a3ebef176f95c762ca34); [Educause: Decompilation as Fair Use](https://www.educause.edu/ir/library/html/erm/29160.html)
- Commentary on fan decomp ports: whether this kind of reverse engineering is fair use "depends heavily on jurisdiction and methodology." Decomp teams document their process and avoid leaked source for that reason — [heldgames explainer](https://heldgames.com/guides/retro-decompilation-recompilation-explained); [Wikipedia: Ship of Harkinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Harkinian)
- Common risk-reduction model: no original assets ship, and users dump their own legally owned copy. Unleashed Recompiled (Sonic Unleashed, X360) works this way — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2025/03/this-new-unofficial-pc-port-of-sonic-unleashed-could-be-the-best-version-of-the-game-yet). So does OpenGOAL (Jak & Daxter, PS2), which requires the user's own ISO — [Overclock3D](https://overclock3d.net/news/software/the-entire-jak-and-daxter-trilogy-is-now-natively-playable-on-pc/); [Time Extension 2026](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2026/04/all-three-mainline-jak-and-daxter-ps2-games-now-have-native-pc-ports)
- Some press coverage overstates the safety of this model. One source claims Sega "will not be able to eliminate the project" because it contains no Sega property. That is a lay opinion, not a legal conclusion, and the decompiled or recompiled code is itself derived from Sega's code — [Notebookcheck (IT)](https://www.notebookcheck.it/Il-classico-gioco-di-culto-Sonic-the-Hedgehog-ottiene-una-porta-non-ufficiale-per-PC.971268.0.html)

### Inferences
- A matching decompilation of Kya (like this repo's `src/b-witch` and `src/EdenLib`) reproduces the expression of Eden's code. Neither EU Art. 6 (interoperability, explicitly not for "substantially similar" programs) nor Sega v. Accolade (the end product was Accolade's own new games) cleanly covers publishing a full decompiled source tree. The most defensible parts are the original port layer (`port/`: Vulkan renderer, PS2 hardware shims, tools), which is independently written code.
- Factors that lower risk in practice: no ISO, assets, ELF or leaked SDK code in the repo or releases; the port loads data from the user's own disc; the project is non-commercial; code is original where possible; there is a clear preservation/interoperability rationale; and the project complies quickly with any notice.
- Sony PS2 SDK libraries statically linked into the original ELF (libgraph, libdma and others) belong to Sony, not Atari. Their decompiled forms are another rights holder's code. Replacing them with original shims (as `src/port/` and the Windows backend appear to do) helps.
- Clean-room reimplementation (one team writes a spec, another team writes new code from it) is legally stronger but much more work. It is what an official licensed product would not need, because the license resolves the issue.

### Gaps
- The verbatim EUR-Lex text and the Italian L. 633/1941 Arts. 64-ter/64-quater were not fetched. Verify the wording.
- No court has ruled directly on a fan "matching decompilation" game port in the EU or the US. The re3 case (below) did not produce a published merits ruling that was found in this research.

## Q3. What precedents exist for takedowns, tolerance, and fan projects becoming official?

### Takeaway
Enforcement depends on the rights holder's choice. Nintendo and Take-Two are aggressive. Take-Two sued re3/reVC timed to its own Definitive Edition. Nintendo's Yuzu settlement shows how monetization (Patreon) and piracy facilitation raise exposure. Many PS2/N64/X360-era decomp ports (OpenGOAL, Ship of Harkinian, Unleashed Recompiled) have been tolerated for years. Sega turned a fan developer (Christian Whitehead) into an official partner. The biggest trigger for a takedown is overlap with the rights holder's own commercial plans.

### Cited Findings
- **Nintendo – Super Mario 64 PC port (2020).** DMCA notices through Wildwood Law Group targeted compiled executables on file hosts, and gameplay videos on YouTube were claimed — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2020/05/nintendo_cracks_down_on_the_super_mario_64_pc_port); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/super-mario-64/pc-port-copyright)
- **Nintendo – Ship of Harkinian (OoT, 2022 onward).** Not taken down, though its legal status is uncertain. It ships no assets and requires the user's ROM — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Harkinian); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-fan-made-pc-port-is-out/1100-6501836/). Twilight Princess decomp reported done in Dec 2025 — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2025/12/zelda-twilight-princess-has-been-decompiled)
- **Nintendo v. Yuzu (Mar 2024).** Tropic Haze paid $2.4M and shut the emulator, source and website about a week after being sued. Nintendo cited circumvention and piracy, including over a million pre-release pirated downloads of TotK. Yuzu's Patreon reportedly made about $30k per month — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/yuzu-creators-pay-2-4-million-to-nintendo-as-settlement-fee/); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2024/03/04/nintendo-yuzu-settlement-payout-emulator-shutdown/)
- **Take-Two v. re3/reVC (GTA III/VC decomp, 2021).** After a DMCA takedown, a counter-notice and reinstatement, Take-Two sued four named developers in California, calling the counter-notices bad faith. GitHub removed the repos again. This happened just before GTA Trilogy Definitive Edition launched — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2021/11/gta-modders-behind-re3-and-revc-fire-back-in-court); [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/72449/take-two-suing-team-that-reverse-engineered-gta-3-and-vice-city-source-code/)
- **Sony – OpenGOAL (Jak 1-3, PS2).** All three mainline games had native PC ports by April 2026, with no reported Sony takedown. The user must supply their own ISO — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2026/04/all-three-mainline-jak-and-daxter-ps2-games-now-have-native-pc-ports); [DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/news/playstation-classic-jak-and-daxter-gets-an-unofficial-native-pc-port/)
- **Sega – Unleashed Recompiled (2025).** Static recompilation of the X360 game with no assets included. No takedown was reported in the sources reviewed — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2025/03/this-new-unofficial-pc-port-of-sonic-unleashed-could-be-the-best-version-of-the-game-yet)
- **Sega – Christian Whitehead.** He built the Retro Engine and fan remakes, then ported Sonic CD, Sonic 1 and Sonic 2 to mobile for Sega. After those sold well he pitched what became Sonic Mania (2017). Sega later relied on fan-scene developers again for Sonic Origins — [Sega Retro](https://segaretro.org/The_Taxman); [Nintendo Life interview](https://3dsvc.nintendolife.com/news/2018/07/sonic_mania_lead_talks_about_the_fan_service_magic_that_makes_the_game_tick); [3DJuegos](https://www.3djuegos.com/noticias-ver/221584/sonic-origins-esta-siendo-desarrollado-por-fans-sega-vuelve/)
- **Nightdive model.** Nightdive restores and publishes classic games under license (System Shock, Doom 64, Quake; more than 100 titles) on its KEX engine and is now part of Atari — [Atari newsroom](https://atari.com/blogs/newsroom/atari-acquires-nightdive-studios)

### Inferences
- For Kya, the relevant rights holder is Atari, not Nintendo. No Atari enforcement record against fan ports was found. Atari's business model of buying and monetizing retro IP cuts both ways: a fan port could be seen as a showcase (the Sonic Mania path) or as competition for a future Nightdive or Digital Eclipse product (the re3 path).
- The Whitehead story is the realistic template: build a high-quality, technically impressive port, show it to the rights holder, and offer to do it officially. Atari's in-house studios make outside licensing less likely, but a skilled team that already has a working engine has value as a contractor or co-developer.

### Gaps
- No public outcome of the re3 lawsuit (settlement or ruling) was found in this session.
- The Re-Logic example named in the brief was not researched. Re-Logic's usual relevance is its open stance toward mods and community tools, which is not a decomp precedent.
- Whether Atari or Nightdive has ever hired or partnered with a fan decomp team is unknown.

## Q4. How would we approach Atari professionally to license an official remaster?

### Takeaway
The practical channel is Atari SA (Paris/New York) business development, or its retro-publishing arms Nightdive and Digital Eclipse, or the revived Infogrames label. The pitch should lead with a working technical demonstration, a clear rights-respecting process, a market rationale, and a proposed deal structure. Expect Atari to want control, likely through an in-house studio or a co-development deal, and expect it to require the public fan release to stay non-commercial or be withdrawn.

### Cited Findings
- Atari describes its acquisitions as a retro-focused growth strategy, and Nightdive's KEX engine is a core asset for bringing classics to modern hardware — [Atari newsroom](https://atari.com/blogs/newsroom/atari-acquires-nightdive-studios); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2023/10/atari-ramps-up-retro-ambitions-with-digital-eclipse-acquisition)
- Digital Eclipse keeps "the freedom to seek out projects with other parties" and has "greater access to Atari's fantastic library" — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2023/10/atari-ramps-up-retro-ambitions-with-digital-eclipse-acquisition)
- Atari relaunched the Infogrames label, which publishes third-party titles, as part of the Thunderful expansion in 2025 — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/07/atari-set-to-acquire-82-of-thunderful-group-ab-as-thunderful-announce-theyre-restructuring/); [FullCleared](https://fullcleared.com/news/atari-revives-infogrames-acquires-popular-game/)
- Sonic Mania precedent: a fan developer moved from fan remakes to paid ports to a new game by pitching after delivering commercially successful work — [Sega Retro](https://segaretro.org/The_Taxman)

### Inferences (practical pitch checklist, synthesized and not sourced)
- **Confirm the rights holder first.** Check the trademark registers (EUIPO, USPTO, INPI) and write to Atari's business development/licensing contact, asking who controls "Kya: Dark Lineage."
- **Pitch contents:**
  1. A short video and build demo of the port (4K, widescreen, 60fps, controller support).
  2. A technical summary showing the port is engine-complete and how the remaster would be done (asset upscaling, new renderer).
  3. An audience signal: wishlists or followers, cult status, the Avatar trivia hook, no existing modern release.
  4. Proposed deal options: (a) a license to the team as developer/publisher on a revenue share; (b) Atari or Infogrames publishes and the team does the work for hire; (c) a co-development hand-off to Nightdive or Digital Eclipse.
  5. A clean-hands statement: no leaked material, assets never redistributed, and a willingness to take the fan build down or restrict it.
- **Other publishers** such as Limited Run Games could add physical editions, but only on top of an Atari license. A third-party publisher can't solve the rights problem.
- **Legal entity.** Set up a company (for example an Italian SRL) before any contract. Steam also requires this (see Q5).
- **Unresolved rights to check:** 2003 music licenses, voice actors' contracts, and any middleware (such as RenderWare or audio libraries) in the original build.

### Gaps
- No public Atari licensing or submission process (portal or email) was found. Atari's IR or contact pages would need to be checked directly.
- No public data was found on Atari's typical license terms or revenue splits for third-party remasters.
- Limited Run Games' partnership terms were not researched.

## Q5. What are the distribution options and their risks (GitHub, itch.io, Steam, donations/Patreon)?

### Takeaway
Without a license, the only realistic channel is free, non-commercial distribution of **source code plus an asset-free build** on GitHub, Codeberg or similar, possibly mirrored on itch.io as a free download, with the user supplying their own disc. Steam is not possible without rights: Valve requires the publishing legal entity to own or have the right to publish the content. Monetization (Patreon, paid builds, ads) greatly increases legal exposure and strengthens a publisher's case, as Yuzu shows. GitHub follows DMCA notice and counter-notice procedures, but counter-notices can provoke a lawsuit (re3).

### Cited Findings
- **Steam.** Valve's onboarding says you cannot publish "content you don't own or have adequate rights to." The company name must be "the legal entity that owns or has right to publish the product on Steam" and must match bank and tax records. Identity verification is required, and there is a $100 app fee per product — [Steamworks onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding)
- **GitHub DMCA dynamics.** GitHub removed re3/reVC after Take-Two's DMCA notice and restored them after an uncontested counter-notice. Take-Two then sued, and GitHub removed them again — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2021/11/gta-modders-behind-re3-and-revc-fire-back-in-court)
- **File hosts and YouTube.** Nintendo targeted compiled SM64 port binaries on file hosts and claimed YouTube videos of them — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2020/05/nintendo_cracks_down_on_the_super_mario_64_pc_port)
- **Monetization risk.** Yuzu's Patreon made about $30k per month, and the case ended with a $2.4M settlement and a full shutdown — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/yuzu-creators-pay-2-4-million-to-nintendo-as-settlement-fee/)
- **Asset-free plus user's own copy.** This is the standard model for tolerated projects (OpenGOAL, Ship of Harkinian, Unleashed Recompiled) — [Wikipedia: Ship of Harkinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Harkinian); [Overclock3D](https://overclock3d.net/news/software/the-entire-jak-and-daxter-trilogy-is-now-natively-playable-on-pc/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended fan-project posture for this repo:**
  - Never commit or attach ISO/ELF/assets.
  - Do extraction at runtime from the user's disc.
  - Release source and a tool-only binary.
  - Don't sell anything, and avoid Patreon or donations tied to the game. If donations exist at all, tie them to general tooling or to individual developers, not to "Kya PC." Even that carries some risk.
  - Don't use Atari or Kya logos in branding beyond describing compatibility.
  - Add a clear disclaimer saying the project is not affiliated with Atari or Eden.
  - Keep a DMCA-response plan, and think hard before counter-noticing given re3.
- **itch.io.** Its terms also require rights to what you upload. A free, asset-free launcher is lower risk than a packaged game, but hosting there gives no legal protection.
- **Jurisdiction.** A developer based in Italy can be reached by EU/Italian civil actions as well as US DMCA processes against US platforms (GitHub, YouTube). EU law has no DMCA-style counter-notice safe harbor for the uploader. Platforms there act under the DSA notice-and-action rules. *This is background knowledge, not sourced in this session.*
- **An official path is the only route to Steam, GOG or consoles,** and the only way to charge money legally.

### Gaps
- itch.io's terms of service were not fetched. Its rights-requirement wording is unverified.
- GOG's curation and rights requirements were not researched.
- There is no data on whether Atari has ever issued DMCA notices on GitHub. GitHub's public dmca repo (github.com/github/dmca) could be searched for "Atari" to check.
